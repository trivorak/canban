# Halliburton Branding Color Palettes

## Primary Colors
Color provides a strong visual link to our brand identity across a wide range of applications. Our flagship color is a bold, confident PMS 186 red—a vibrant color statement that we make to the world. Always use red at 100%. Never use a screen (lesser percentage) or tint of it. Percentages of red reproduce as pink, which is not the brand image we want to project.
Large fields of a single color help make our communications striking and impactful. The dominant color red also helps photos stand out.
**DO NOT** use colors other than those in the Halliburton color palette.
**DO NOT** use multiply blend modes or transparency, or change opacity of the colors.
### Color Matching
The CMYK mixes are based on media tests using coated stock. The mixes may need to be adjusted to suit your needs based on the paper stock that you are using.
### Brand Colors and Psychology 
Our brand colors stand for something. We don’t just use colors without thought or strategy behind them, we choose certain colors because they help evoke specific emotions, and/or actions.

## Secondary Pallete and Fuctional Colors
A **secondary** palette allows you to expand and modernize the brand’s look and feel.
Neutrals include shades of grey, typically used for backgrounds, text colors, drop shadows, etc.Functional colors help create a consistent and clear user experience across the brand’s products and services. 
**Functional colors** should be used to for charts and tables in PowerPoint and other elements you want to call attention to or highlight.  
**DO NOT** use multiply blend modes or transparency, or change opacity of the colors.

## Support and Utility Color Pallete
A support palette allows you to expand and modernize the brand’s look and feel.
The **support colors** can be used to complement brand visuals and draw attention to certain elements. Always use the support colors sparingly and carefully, so they do not overshadow or clash with the primary colors. The primary colors should always be the most dominant and consistent in the design. 
**Utility colors** are used to communicate purpose. Used consistently over time, they help convey messages to the user. For example, on the website, yellow is used to call out announcements and similar time-sensitive information, the red is used for the primary call to action buttons and links.
**DO NOT** use multiply blend modes or transparency, or change the opacity of the colors
